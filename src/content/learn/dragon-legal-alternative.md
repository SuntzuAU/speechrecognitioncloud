Lawyers were some of Dragon's most loyal users for a simple reason: legal work is writing, and most lawyers talk faster than they type. Letters, memos, briefs, file notes, emails to opposing counsel — dictation turns a day of typing into an hour of talking.

That's why the changes to the Dragon Legal line matter. If you use Dragon Legal today, you need a plan within the next year. This guide sets out what is happening, the realistic options, and where each one fits.

## Can Speech Recognition Cloud handle legal terminology and case names?

**Mostly, yes. Speech Recognition Cloud Professional uses an Ultra Accuracy recognition model that handles general legal language well from the first sentence, and you can add your own vocabulary for the names and terms it doesn't know.**

Two tools cover the rest:

- **Custom vocabulary** — add party names, opposing counsel, judges, case names and unusual terms. It has a character limit, so it's best used for the words that trip recognition up most often.
- **[Text replacements](/support/text-replacements/)** — unlimited. Map a short spoken phrase to a longer block of text: a standard signature, a governing-law clause, a without-prejudice header, a citation you use every week. This is the closest equivalent to Dragon auto-texts, and it's where most switching lawyers rebuild their shortcuts.

Where to be realistic: a lawyer who has used Dragon Legal for years with a heavily trained profile may still see better accuracy on rare, specialist terms, because Dragon has adapted to their voice and their vocabulary. Speech Recognition Cloud doesn't build a voice profile, so it won't learn your voice over time — the accuracy you get on day one is the accuracy you get on day 365. For general legal dictation, the Ultra Accuracy model is designed to work well without any setup. For deep specialist vocabulary, test it on your own work before deciding.

## Is cloud dictation confidential enough for client work?

**Speech Recognition Cloud stores no dictated audio or text. Audio is transcribed and discarded, the text is returned to your computer, and nothing is used to train AI models.** The only customer data held is the email address you sign in with.

The professional rules put the judgement on you. Under ABA Model Rule 1.6, lawyers must make reasonable efforts to prevent unauthorised disclosure of client information, and that includes understanding the technology vendors you use. State bars have issued their own guidance on cloud services, and the American Bar Association's [Formal Opinion 512](https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf) (July 2024) addresses lawyers' use of generative AI tools.

Two points for that assessment:

- **Standard dictation is not generative AI.** It types the words you speak; it doesn't draft, summarise or invent.
- **AI Modes are optional.** The Professional plan includes AI Modes that can rework dictated text. If you use them on client material, treat them as you would any generative AI tool under Formal Opinion 512 and your state's guidance. Many lawyers simply leave them off.

We don't describe the product as compliant with any rule or opinion, because compliance is a property of how a firm uses a tool, not of the software itself. More detail is on [voice dictation for lawyers](/legal).

## Where is Dragon still the better choice for lawyers?

Being straight about it, Dragon Professional v16 is the better fit if:

1. **You need to dictate offline.** Speech Recognition Cloud needs an internet connection. Dragon v16 runs entirely on your computer.
2. **You run your computer by voice.** Dragon's command, macro and scripting capability is far more developed. If you navigate Word, format documents and control applications by voice, Speech Recognition Cloud is not a replacement for that.
3. **Your firm prohibits cloud processing.** Some firms and clients rule out any cloud service for client material.
4. **You have years of trained profile data** on deep specialist vocabulary and your accuracy depends on it.

It's also Windows only, like Dragon. There is no Mac version.

For everyone else — most lawyers and legal staff dictating letters, memos, file notes and emails — Speech Recognition Cloud does the core job with no training, no serial numbers, no profile to rebuild on a new computer, and at a fraction of the cost.
